# Annotated bibliography

**What this file is:** the source list for `corpus.md`. Keys match the `[Author Year]` citations used there.

**How sources were checked:** every entry was checked on 2026-10-29 against PubMed metadata (PMID/DOI) and the abstract. Where a source is not indexed in PubMed, the entry says how it was checked instead.

**What the tags mean:**
* **Design:** MA = meta-analysis · SR = systematic review · RCT = randomised controlled trial · WS = within-subject (one person's limbs compared against each other) · NR = narrative review · Delphi = expert-consensus study
* **Populations:** UT = untrained · T = trained
* **Measurement:** MRI = imaging volume or area · US = ultrasound thickness
* **Statistics:** SMD = standardised mean difference

## Umbrella / position stands

**[Currier 2026]**
* Currier BS, et al. ACSM Position Stand: Resistance training prescription for muscle function, hypertrophy, and physical performance in healthy adults: an overview of reviews. *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 2026;58(4):851–872. PMID 41843416. DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897.
* **Design:** overview of 137 systematic reviews (>30k participants).
* **Findings:**
  * Hypertrophy improves with ≥10 sets per week and with eccentric overload.
  * Training to failure, equipment type, set structure and periodization did not consistently improve outcomes.
* **Use:** our best current umbrella reference.

**[Iversen 2021]**
* Iversen VM, Norum M, Schoenfeld BJ, Fimland MS. No time to lift? Designing time-efficient training programs for strength and hypertrophy: a narrative review. *Sports Med* 2021;51(10):2079–2095. PMID 34125411. DOI 10.1007/s40279-021-01490-1.
* **Recommendations:**
  * ≥4 sets per muscle per week at 6–15RM.
  * Prioritise bilateral compounds.
  * Supersets, drop sets and rest-pause roughly halve session time.
* NR — the most directly relevant paper for busy dads.

## 1. Exercise selection

### Long muscle lengths / stretch

**[Maeo 2021]**
* Maeo S, et al. Greater hamstrings muscle hypertrophy but similar damage protection after training at long versus short muscle lengths. *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 2021;53(4):825–837. PMID 33009197. DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000002523.
* **Design:** WS, UT (n=20), MRI.
* **Finding:** seated leg curl beat prone leg curl for whole hamstrings (+14% vs +9%), driven by the two-joint heads.

**[Maeo 2023]**
* Maeo S, et al. Triceps brachii hypertrophy is substantially greater after elbow extension training performed in the overhead versus neutral arm position. *Eur J Sport Sci* 2023;23(7):1240–1250. PMID 35819335. DOI 10.1080/17461391.2022.2100279.
* **Design:** WS (n=21), MRI.
* **Finding:** overhead beat pushdown for the long head (+28.5% vs +19.6%) and whole triceps (+19.9% vs +13.9%).

**[Kinoshita 2023]**
* Kinoshita M, Maeo S, et al. Triceps surae muscle hypertrophy is greater after standing versus seated calf-raise training. *Front Physiol* 2023;14:1272106. PMID 38156065. DOI 10.3389/fphys.2023.1272106.
* **Design:** n=14, MRI.
* **Finding:** standing calf raises grew the gastrocnemius far more; soleus growth was similar.

**[Kobayashi 2026]**
* Kobayashi Y, Maeo S, et al. Exercise-specific preferential hypertrophy of individual elbow flexor muscles after incline versus preacher dumbbell curl training. *J Sports Sci* 2026. PMID 42829982. DOI 10.1080/02640414.2026.2741751.
* **Design:** MRI.
* **Finding:**
  * Incline curl favoured the biceps.
  * Preacher curl favoured brachialis and brachioradialis.
  * Total growth was equal.

**[Varovic 2025]**
* Varovic D, Wolf M, Schoenfeld BJ, et al. Does muscle length influence regional hypertrophy? A systematic review and meta-analysis. *Int J Sports Med* 2025;46(14):1027–1036. PMID 40570881. DOI 10.1055/a-2615-4935.
* **Design:** Bayesian MA, 12 studies.
* **Finding:** trivial advantage for longer muscle lengths (SMD 0.05–0.09).

**[Pedrosa 2026 review]**
* Pedrosa GF, Pereira MR, Kassiano W. The interplay between muscle length, range of motion, and exercise selection: a review. *Sports Med Int Open* 2026;10. PMID 41777276. DOI 10.1055/a-2733-7605.
* NR — benefits require meaningful external torque in the lengthened position.

**[Warneke 2024]**
* Warneke K, et al. Effects of chronic static stretching on maximal strength and muscle hypertrophy: a systematic review and meta-analysis with meta-regression. *Sports Med Open* 2024;10(1):45. PMID 38637473. DOI 10.1186/s40798-024-00706-8.
* **Design:** 42 studies.
* **Finding:** d≈0.20, significant only at high stretching doses.

**[Warneke 2023a]**
* Warneke K, et al. Comparison of the effects of long-lasting static stretching and hypertrophy training on maximal strength, muscle thickness and flexibility in the plantar flexors. *Eur J Appl Physiol* 2023;123(8):1773–1787. PMID 37029826. DOI 10.1007/s00421-023-05184-6.
* **Finding:** 1 h per day of stretching matched calf training for muscle thickness. Not practical.

**[Warneke 2023b]**
* Warneke K, et al. Influence of long-lasting static stretching intervention on functional and morphological parameters in the plantar flexors: a randomized controlled trial. *J Strength Cond Res* 2023;37(10):1993–2001. PMID 37318350. DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000004513.
* **Design:** MRI.
* **Finding:** small cross-sectional-area effect (d 0.16–0.30).

**[Wohlann 2024]**
* Wohlann T, Warneke K, et al. Influence of 8 weeks of supervised static stretching or resistance training of pectoral major muscles on maximal strength, muscle thickness and range of motion. *Eur J Appl Physiol* 2024;124(6):1885–1893. PMID 38240811. DOI 10.1007/s00421-023-05413-y.
* **Design:** US.
* **Finding:** 15 min/day of stretching, 4 days/week, matched 5×12 resistance training.

### Range of motion / lengthened partials

**[Pallarés 2021]**
* Pallarés JG, et al. Effects of range of motion on resistance training adaptations: a systematic review and meta-analysis. *Scand J Med Sci Sports* 2021;31(10):1866–1881. PMID 34170576. DOI 10.1111/sms.14006.
* **Finding:** full ROM beat partial ROM for strength (ES 0.56) and lower-limb hypertrophy (0.88).
* **Caveat:** partials were not split by muscle length.

**[Schoenfeld & Grgic 2020]**
* Schoenfeld BJ, Grgic J. Effects of range of motion on muscle development during resistance training interventions: a systematic review. *SAGE Open Med* 2020;8:2050312120901559. PMID 32030125. DOI 10.1177/2050312120901559.
* **Finding:** full ROM favours lower-body hypertrophy; upper-body data conflict.

**[Kassiano 2023b]**
* Kassiano W, Costa B, Nunes JP, Ribeiro AS, Schoenfeld BJ, Cyrino ES. Which ROMs lead to Rome? A systematic review of the effects of range of motion on muscle hypertrophy. *J Strength Cond Res* 2023;37(5):1135–1144. PMID 36662126. DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000004415.
* **Finding:** full ROM and initial-range partials both beat final-range partials.

**[Wolf 2023]**
* Wolf M, Androulakis-Korakakis P, Fisher J, Schoenfeld B, Steele J. Partial vs full range of motion resistance training: a systematic review and meta-analysis. *Int J Strength Cond* 2023;3(1). DOI 10.47206/ijsc.v3i1.182.
* Not in PubMed; checked on the journal site.
* **Finding:**
  * Overall SMD 0.12 favouring full ROM.
  * In the lengthened-partial subgroup, SMD was −0.28 (CI crosses 0).
* **Caveat:** number of included studies not confirmed.

**[Pedrosa 2022]**
* Pedrosa GF, et al. Partial range of motion training elicits favorable improvements in muscular adaptations when carried out at long muscle lengths. *Eur J Sport Sci* 2022;22(8):1250–1260. PMID 33977835. DOI 10.1080/17461391.2021.1927199.
* **Design:** UT women (n=45), knee extension.
* **Finding:** initial-range partials matched or beat full ROM at some measurement sites.

**[Pedrosa 2023]**
* Pedrosa GF, et al. Training in the initial range of motion promotes greater muscle adaptations than at final in the arm curl. *Sports (Basel)* 2023;11(2):39. PMID 36828324. DOI 10.3390/sports11020039.
* **Design:** WS, UT women (n=19).
* **Finding:** distal-site advantage for the initial range; summed area did not differ.

**[Kassiano 2023a]**
* Kassiano W, et al. Greater gastrocnemius muscle hypertrophy after partial range of motion training performed at long muscle lengths. *J Strength Cond Res* 2023;37(9):1746–1753. PMID 37015016. DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000004460.
* **Design:** UT women (n=42).
* **Finding:** medial gastrocnemius grew +15.2% with lengthened partials vs +6.7% with full ROM.

**[Gschneidner 2025]**
* Gschneidner D, Carlson L, Steele J, et al. The effects of lengthened-partial range of motion resistance training of the limbs on arm and thigh muscle area: a multi-site randomised trial. *J Sports Sci* 2025;43(23):2963–2976. PMID 41055237. DOI 10.1080/02640414.2025.2567805.
* **Design:** pre-registered, 15 sites, n≈297.
* **Finding:** lengthened partials were not better than full ROM; the pre-registered equivalence test did not pass at its threshold.
* The largest trial on the question.

**[Wolf 2025]**
* Wolf M, et al. Lengthened partial repetitions elicit similar muscular adaptations as full range of motion repetitions during resistance training in trained individuals. *PeerJ* 2025;13:e18904. PMID 39959841. DOI 10.7717/peerj.18904.
* **Design:** T (n=30).
* **Finding:** Bayes factors 0.16–0.3, i.e. evidence of no difference.

**[Plotkin 2026]**
* Plotkin DL, et al. Acute molecular and chronic vastus lateralis adaptations to lengthened partial versus full range of motion resistance training in trained men. *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 2026. PMID 42841600. DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000004183.
* **Design:** T, MRI.
* **Finding:** no difference.

**[Goto 2019]**
* Goto M, et al. Partial range of motion exercise is effective for facilitating muscle hypertrophy and function through sustained intramuscular hypoxia in young trained men. *J Strength Cond Res* 2019;33(5):1286–1294. PMID 31034463. DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000002051.
* **Finding:** mid-range partials gave +48.7% vs +28.2%.
* **Caveat:** an outlier with implausible magnitude; not acted on.

**[Larsen 2025]**
* Larsen S, Swinton PA, et al. Resistance training beyond momentary failure: the effects of past-failure partials on muscle hypertrophy in the gastrocnemius. *Front Psychol* 2025;16:1494323. PMID 39995432. DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1494323.
* **Finding:** lengthened partials added after failure gave +9.6% vs +6.7%.
* **Caveat:** the partials added volume.

**[Goli 2026]**
* Goli A, et al. Does performing partial repetitions beyond momentary failure enhance muscle hypertrophy in volume-load-equated calf-raise training? *Int J Exerc Sci* 2026;19(4):4003. PMID 42006449. DOI 10.70252/IJES2026403.
* **Finding:** equal growth when volume load was equated (8% vs 8%).

**[McMahon 2026]**
* McMahon G, et al. Moderate intensity resistance training with partial ROM at long muscle lengths elicits similar hypertrophy… *J Strength Cond Res* 2026. PMID 42392615. DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000005561.
* **Finding:** lengthened partials at 55% 1RM matched full ROM at 80% 1RM.

**[Wolf 2025b]**
* Wolf M, et al. Does longer-muscle length resistance training cause greater longitudinal growth in humans? A systematic review. *Sports Med Health Sci* 2025;8(1):34–42. PMID 41646176. DOI 10.1016/j.smhs.2025.03.001.
* **Finding:** mixed results; fascicle-length measurement is questionable.

### Equipment / stability

* Haugen ME, Vårvik FT, Larsen S, Haugen AS, van den Tillaar R, Bjørnsen T. Effect of free-weight vs. machine-based strength training on maximal strength, hypertrophy and jump performance: a systematic review and meta-analysis. *BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil* 2023;15(1):103. PMID 37582807. DOI 10.1186/s13102-023-00713-4.

### Regional hypertrophy

**[Kubo 2019]**
* Kubo K, Ikebukuro T, Yata H. Effects of squat training with different depths on lower limb muscle volumes. *Eur J Appl Physiol* 2019;119(9):1933–1942. PMID 31230110. DOI 10.1007/s00421-019-04181-y.
* **Design:** MRI.
* **Finding:**
  * Full squats grew glutes and adductors more than half squats.
  * Knee extensors grew equally.
  * Rectus femoris and hamstrings did not grow in either group.

**[Bloomquist 2013]**
* Bloomquist K, et al. Effect of range of motion in heavy load squatting on muscle and tendon adaptations. *Eur J Appl Physiol* 2013;113(8):2133–2142. PMID 23604798. DOI 10.1007/s00421-013-2642-7.
* **Finding:** deep squats gave greater front-thigh cross-sectional area.

**[Plotkin 2023]**
* Plotkin DL, Rodas MA, Vigotsky AD, et al. Hip thrust and back squat training elicit similar gluteus muscle hypertrophy and transfer similarly to the deadlift. *Front Physiol* 2023;14:1279170. PMID 37877099. DOI 10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170.
* **Design:** MRI.
* **Finding:**
  * Glute growth was similar between the two lifts.
  * Squats also grew quads and adductors.
  * EMG did not predict growth.

**[Zabaleta-Korta 2021]**
* Zabaleta-Korta A, et al. The role of exercise selection in regional muscle hypertrophy: a randomized controlled trial. *J Sports Sci* 2021;39(20):2298–2304. PMID 34743671. DOI 10.1080/02640414.2021.1929736.
* **Finding:** leg extension grew every region of the rectus femoris; Smith squat did not.

**[Zabaleta-Korta 2023]**
* Zabaleta-Korta A, et al. Regional hypertrophy: the effect of exercises at long and short muscle lengths in recreationally trained women. *J Hum Kinet* 2023;87:259–270. PMID 37559762. DOI 10.5114/jhk/163561.
* **Finding:** preacher curl grew the distal site; incline curl gave no significant growth.
* **Caveat:** conflicts with [Kobayashi 2026].

**[Nunes 2020]**
* Nunes JP, et al. Different foot positioning during calf training to induce portion-specific gastrocnemius muscle hypertrophy. *J Strength Cond Res* 2020;34(8):2347–2351. PMID 32735428. DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000003674.

**[Brandão 2020]**
* Brandão L, et al. Varying the order of combinations of single- and multi-joint exercises differentially affects resistance training adaptations. *J Strength Cond Res* 2020;34(5):1254–1263. PMID 32149887. DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000003550.
* **Finding:**
  * Bench press alone did not significantly grow total triceps.
  * It grew the lateral head; the long head needed extensions.
* Key support for counting indirect sets at less than 1.

**[Mannarino 2021]**
* Mannarino P, et al. Single-joint exercise results in higher hypertrophy of elbow flexors than multijoint exercise. *J Strength Cond Res* 2021;35(10):2677–2681. PMID 31268995. DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000003234.
* **Design:** WS, n=10.
* **Finding:** curls +11.1% vs rows +5.2%.

**[Gentil 2013]**
* Gentil P, et al. Effect of adding single-joint exercises to a multi-joint exercise resistance-training program on strength and hypertrophy in untrained subjects. *Appl Physiol Nutr Metab* 2013;38(3):341–344. PMID 23537028. DOI 10.1139/apnm-2012-0176.
* **Design:** UT.
* **Finding:** no added benefit from single-joint work.

**[de França 2015]**
* de França HS, et al. The effects of adding single-joint exercises to a multi-joint exercise resistance training program on upper body muscle strength and size in trained men. *Appl Physiol Nutr Metab* 2015;40(8):822–826. PMID 26244600. DOI 10.1139/apnm-2015-0109.
* **Finding:** no difference, but size was measured by circumference only (weak).

### Proximity to failure

**[Robinson 2024]**

### Velocity / autoregulation
